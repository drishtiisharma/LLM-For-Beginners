# Understanding How LLMs Actually “Think”

Let's explore local AI a little more and understand how LLMs behave internally.
I experimented with running different local models using Ollama and compared how smaller and larger models behave under different parameters like:
- temperature
- token limits (`num_predict`)
- model size (`1b vs 8b`)

At first, I thought increasing temperature simply makes AI “more creative.” But after testing multiple prompts, I realized there’s much more hidden depth to it.

# What I Explored Today

### 1. Understanding Temperature

Temperature controls randomness in token selection.

- Low temperature (`0.2 – 0.5`) → safer, focused, predictable outputs
- High temperature (`0.8 – 1.5+`) → more creative, unexpected, and sometimes chaotic outputs

One thing became very noticeable while experimenting:

- Larger models stayed creative while still making sense.
- Smaller models became random and unstable much faster.

And that’s when I finally understood something important:

> Higher temperature does NOT make a model smarter.
> It only changes how adventurous its word selection becomes.


### 2. Comparing Small vs Large Models

I compared:
- `llama3.2:1b`
- `llama3.1:8b`

And honestly, the differences became surprisingly visible very quickly.

#### Smaller Models
- faster and lightweight
- good for quick tasks
- lower RAM usage

But:
- lose coherence faster
- drift away from the original topic more easily
- struggle more with longer conversations

#### Larger Models
- slower
- heavier on RAM
- much more capable overall

They:
- maintained tone and context better
- handled longer responses more naturally
- produced more structured and meaningful outputs

One thing that stood out:
larger models handled higher temperatures much better without completely losing coherence.


### 3. Understanding Token Limits (`num_predict`)

Today I also explored:
```text
num_predict
```

This controls how many tokens the model is allowed to generate.

In simple terms:
> how long the response can become.

#### Small Token Limits
- shorter responses
- more direct answers
- faster outputs

#### Large Token Limits
- longer explanations
- more detailed responses
- extended generations

And again, model size mattered a lot.

Larger models handled longer outputs much better.

Smaller models often:
- repeated themselves
- drifted off-topic
- forgot earlier context
- or got cut off awkwardly mid-thought


# The Simple Understanding I Finally Reached

After all these experiments, this became my mental model:

- Temperature = creativity/randomness
- `num_predict` = response length
- Model size = coherence + contextual understanding

And suddenly, LLM behavior started making much more sense.
